Gross vs net rental yield in Dubai: how to calculate your return

By Ilinka, Real Estate Consultant at Capital Solutions Group
16 October 2026 · 5 minute read
Short answer

Gross rental yield is the yearly rent divided by the property price, times 100. Net rental yield subtracts yearly costs such as service charges, maintenance and management fees from the rent before dividing by the price. Net yield is the figure that shows what you actually earn.

The two formulas

Gross yield = yearly rent ÷ property price × 100

Net yield = (yearly rent minus yearly costs) ÷ property price × 100

Gross yield is quick and useful for comparing listings. Net yield is more honest, because it accounts for the costs every owner pays. When someone quotes a yield, always ask which one it is.

A worked example

Here is an illustrative two bedroom apartment. The figures are examples to show the method, not a forecast for any real property.

ItemAmount
Purchase priceAED 1,500,000
Yearly rentAED 110,000
Yearly service charges and costsAED 18,000
Gross yield7.3%
Net yield6.1%

The gross yield is 110,000 ÷ 1,500,000 × 100 = 7.3%. After costs, the owner keeps AED 92,000 a year, so the net yield is 92,000 ÷ 1,500,000 × 100 = 6.1%. You can try your own numbers in the yield calculator.

Costs that reduce your yield

  • Service charges. Paid yearly to maintain the building and shared facilities, usually charged per square foot. They vary widely between buildings.
  • Property management. If an agency manages tenants for you, expect a fee, often a percentage of the rent.
  • Maintenance and repairs. Air conditioning servicing, appliances and repainting between tenants.
  • Vacancy. Weeks without a tenant reduce the rent you actually receive in a year.
  • Purchase costs. The 4% Dubai Land Department fee and other buying costs. Some investors add these to the price when calculating yield, which gives a more conservative figure.

Reading yield figures carefully

A high yield is not automatically a better investment. It can reflect a higher vacancy risk, an older building with rising service charges or an area where prices are growing slowly. Compare yield alongside capital growth potential, the quality of the building and how easily the unit could be resold.

For off plan property, rent only starts after handover, so the yield is an estimate based on what similar finished units achieve today. Treat any projected yield as an estimate with assumptions, never as a guarantee.

Frequently asked questions

What is a good rental yield in Dubai?

It depends on the area, building and property type. Compare net yield rather than gross yield, and weigh it against vacancy risk, service charges and capital growth potential.

What is the difference between gross and net yield?

Gross yield uses the yearly rent only. Net yield subtracts yearly costs such as service charges, management and maintenance before dividing by the price, so it is lower and more realistic.

Do I pay income tax on rental income in Dubai?

The UAE does not levy personal income tax on individuals, but you may owe tax on the income in your home country. Check with a tax adviser where you are resident.
